Some pages explain themselves all at once. Every corner has a label, every button wants attention, and every empty space is asked to do a job.

There is another possibility: leave a little room for someone to notice something on their own.

![A dark, geometric map of Lagos behind the centered profile on Nosa’s portfolio.](/images/lagos/dark-desktop.png)

## A small invitation

On this portfolio, the map is the invitation. At first it looks like a field of little marks. Look again and the lagoon appears. Move the pointer and the shapes respond.

None of that needs to be understood before the page is useful. **Writings, résumé, projects.** The important destinations are still there. The extra detail is something to discover along the way.

That is an interesting balance to aim for: an interface that is clear when you need it, and curious when you have a moment to spare.

> A detail can be worth adding even when it does not need to announce itself.

## What earns its place?

A useful question for any decorative element is not just “does it look good?” It is “what happens to everything around it?”

- Does the motion make the text harder to read?
- Does a label explain something, or repeat what is already obvious?
- Can the page still work when the effect is turned off?
- Is there a natural point at which the interaction comes to rest?

These questions tend to lead toward smaller gestures. A brief trail instead of a constant animation. A faint geographic label instead of a legend. A single icon instead of another panel.

Light mode changes the atmosphere, but it should not change the hierarchy. The name remains the first thing to read. The navigation remains easy to find. The map remains a background.

### A short checklist for the next pass

1. Read the page on a small screen.
2. Try it with motion reduced.
3. Follow a link, then use the browser’s back button.
4. Read a long paragraph without touching the map at all.

If the page feels good in those quieter moments, the interactive parts have a better chance of feeling intentional.

## Keeping the writing simple

The publishing side follows the same principle. A small Markdown file is enough to give a piece a title, a summary, and a place on the site:

```yaml
title: "A little room for curiosity"
description: "A note about making space."
date: 2026-10-17
draft: false
```

The rest is just writing. A `##` introduces a section; a link connects an idea to another page; an image gives the reader a pause. The layout takes care of the presentation.
